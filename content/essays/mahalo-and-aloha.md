title: Watching Mahalo and Aloha Fly Home
date: 2026-09-14
summary: Two birds over Nairobi at dusk, and a question about borders, passports and what freedom really means.

It was around 7 p.m. in Nairobi. The sun had already set, and it was starting to get dark. Then I saw two birds flying at a relatively high speed. They were following each other, one leading (let us call it Mahalo) and the other following (let us call this one Aloha). "Look at them heading to their sleeping places." My mind continued, "How impressive that they can still figure out where they live at night." These were the thoughts running through my mind when I saw Mahalo and Aloha.

I continued thinking and wondering what the birds had eaten for dinner, if such a thing exists in their world. I wondered what they had spent their whole day doing. Where were Mahalo and Aloha coming from? Why were they heading home that late?

And then I started admiring the lives of Mahalo and Aloha. This is common for me. I do admire the lives of birds and trees and many other things. Matthew 6:26 in the Bible mentions, "Look at the birds of the air: they neither sow nor reap nor gather into barns…." Sometimes I just wonder how our lives would be if we did not have to care or worry about the future, just like Mahalo and Aloha. We would just wake up and go outside, and in the evening find where our humble abodes are, just like the two birds.

If this was the world we somehow found ourselves in, economics classes, rich and poor countries, social classes, race - might never have existed. Not even passports, visas, and work permits would exist in my imagined world. We would be living like Mahalo and Aloha. We would have the liberty to travel long distances without having to show our passports and visas before being allowed to cross borders. As I thought about this, I remembered something I had read a few days earlier about birds and their extraordinary migrations. A 2018 article by [BirdLife International](https://www.birdlife.org/news/2018/06/27/migration-marathons-7-unbelievable-bird-journeys/) described the journeys of seven birds that travelled thousands of kilometres across countries and continents. For example, a Bar-tailed Godwit (*scientific name: Limosa lapponica*) traveled a distance of 11,000 km from Alaska to New Zealand nonstop. Impressive, right? I sure bet the bird did not know about passports or boundaries and was not asked for a visa by anyone or anything along the way.

But then I stopped myself. Mahalo and Aloha were not flying without direction. They were heading somewhere. Somewhere they call home, a nest, a branch, a place they return to every single night. Maybe freedom was never really the absence of belonging. Maybe it was just belonging without a passport. Even birds have territories. Even birds fight for a place to call theirs. Perhaps what I envied was not that Mahalo and Aloha had no borders, but that their borders did not need permission, and that they were natural rather than bureaucratic. I thought of my own passport, tucked away in a drawer somewhere, full of stamps that decide where I am allowed to exist. I remembered standing in a visa line once, waiting for a stranger behind a glass window to decide whether my reasons for travelling were good enough. I do not think Mahalo and Aloha have ever had to explain themselves to anyone. I do not think they have ever had to prove that they will come back.

And yet here I was, free to simply turn around and walk back to my own nest, no explanation required. It was getting cold, and I had to get back to my room. I hoped that Mahalo and Aloha made it to their nests, or wherever they were going. I found myself wondering whether we can ever truly know if they are free, or whether we simply project our own longing for freedom onto them. Maybe their lives are not actually better than ours. Maybe we only envy the parts of their lives we happen to see.
